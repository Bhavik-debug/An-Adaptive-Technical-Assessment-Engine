## The independence problem, stated first because it bounds everything else

- Answers were **generated** by: `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-lightning-30b-a3b`
- Reference labels were **assigned** by: `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-lightning-30b-a3b`

**These are the same model.** The configured provider (`nvidia`) serves every
capability tier from one model, so the answer generator, the grader under test
and this reference labeller are all the same weights. A QWK computed between
the grader and these labels measures *agreement between two prompts to one
model*, not validity against an independent judgement, and a shared blind spot
is invisible to it.

This is a limitation of the available infrastructure, not a design choice, and
it is the main reason the Day-18 label passes are retained and reported
alongside: those were produced by a *different* model family, so despite their
own contamination they are the less circular reference for grader validity.

## Score distribution

| Band | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Count | 1 | 2 | 17 | 98 | 2 |

- modal band **3**, holding **81.7%** of all rows
- populated bands: **5/5**, effective range **(0, 4)**

**This is severe central tendency and it is the biggest weakness of this
pass.** More than half the dataset sits in one band, so the labels carry very
little information about the *ordering* of those rows. A quadratically
weighted kappa needs disagreement in the reference marginal to have anything
to divide by; with a marginal this concentrated the statistic is close to
degenerate and will read low **regardless of how good the grader is**.

It is reported rather than corrected. Re-binning, rescaling or re-prompting
the labeller until the histogram looked better would be fitting the yardstick
to the thing it measures.

The gap between those first two numbers is itself a finding: this labeller
readily identifies *missing* content and rarely identifies *wrong* content,
which is consistent with the Day-18 observation that the generator writes
fluent, mostly-correct prose - and also consistent with a labeller that is
reluctant to call its own model family wrong. The two cannot be separated with
the evidence available here.
